## Rendering (X.2)

RealityKit, physically based, in an `ARView` (non-AR on the Mac) wrapped for SwiftUI: it has the
skybox, image-based lighting and `snapshot` the dev options need; `SceneEvents.Update` drives the
frame (`DriveSession.advance` → interpolated `CarPose` → car, camera, sun).

- **Textures** are drawn at launch, off the main thread, by `ProceduralTextures` (tileable value
  noise, fractal): asphalt (aggregate, stones, polished patches), grass, gravel, kerbs (red/white;
  yellow/black for high kerbs), guard rail steel, tyre walls, the start line's chequer; each with
  albedo (sRGB values: real asphalt ≈ 0.3), roughness and a normal map, sampled trilinear with 16×
  anisotropy. No image files.
- **Light**: an equirectangular sky (gradient, clouds, the sun's disc) is both the skybox and the
  environment light; a directional sun (`ProceduralTextures.sunDirection`) with shadows follows the
  car (`.fixed` orthographic projection, 60 m across: the `.automatic` one cast none);
  `ToneMappingComponent` on macOS 27. Bloom (`BloomComponent`) left out: it drew ghost blobs.
- **Geometry**: `TrackMesh` follows the web's `TrackView.ts` (same layers and heights above the
  road) from the same compiled `Track`; every quad is wound counter-clockwise round its own normal
  (RealityKit's front face), else it is lit from behind and turns black.
- **Car**: `CarEntity`, the web's car rebuilt in rounded parts with clear-coat paint, carbon, a
  cockpit (padded rim, headrest, helmet, steering wheel with display and shift lights, turned with
  the steering), the halo (a hoop of 20 bars from its rear mounts to the centre pillar), mirrors and
  wheels (tyre, rim, spokes) that steer and spin.
- **Cameras**: the web's `CAMERA_PARAMS` (`CameraParams`): chase with yaw smoothing, Z looks back,
  the field of view widens with speed. First person is a **real onboard shot** (owner, 2026-11-22,
  after a TV onboard): low, just behind and above the helmet (0.12 m behind the centre, 1.17 m up,
  72° FOV, near plane 3 cm), looking through the halo at the whole car: both front tyres, mirrors,
  the steering wheel, the helmet's top. The web's view is higher and wider (it has no cockpit).

Elevated circuits (Monaco, São Paulo) drive on their heights; their land comes with the scenery
(X.5), so until then the ground is a flat plane below the lowest road.

## HUD and Time trial (X.3)

The web's HUD in SwiftUI over the 3D view, every panel in Liquid Glass: the timing strip top centre
(`TimingHud` ports `Hud.ts`: sector colours purple/green/yellow, grey when the lap is void, the
chip with the gap to the best lap for 3 s), the circuit and mode top left with the F3 telemetry
under it (open from the start in Test), the minimap bottom left, keys and speed at the bottom.
Models assign only changed values, so SwiftUI redraws little at 120 Hz.

Time trial: the own best lap of the session comes back as a see-through car with no shadow
(`Ghost`, `CarEntity.makeGhost`); every valid lap goes to this Mac's records (`LocalRecords`: the
user defaults under the web's key `altf1.timeTrial.best`, circuit name → ticks), shown in the track
menu (`best 1:23.456`; the other modes show the lap's length from `index.json`). Test's bot (B)
comes with the bots (X.4).

## The car (X.3+, the owner's photos)

Every car has the same body, only its `Livery` (body and accent colour) differs. The bodywork is
lofted (`Loft`: superellipse sections, monotone-cubic interpolation, smooth normals): the spine (a
long thin nose, the bulkhead at 0.76 m, the open cockpit down to 0.45 m, the airbox at 1.02 m behind
the head, the engine cover to the gearbox), the sidepods (wide at the inlets, narrowing and dropping
to the rear), the cockpit's two walls, the floor. On it: front wing (main plane, two flaps,
endplates, pylons), rear wing (endplates, main plane, flap, beam wing, pylon), the red rain light
and the endplates' lights, suspension arms, sidepod and airbox inlets, T-cam, fin, mirrors, the halo,
18-inch wheels (yellow band, dark cover), and the driver: helmet and visor, arms on the steering
wheel (display, shift lights), legs under the bulkhead, in the team's colour, darker. The bodies'
meshes are built once and shared by every car.

**More realistic, and damage on the car** (C.1, 2026-11-23, the owner: "more realistic; when there
is damage I want to see it, maybe with pieces flying off"):

- The wings are lofted aerofoils (`CarEntity.wingElement`: elliptical sections along the span):
  the front wing a carbon main plane and three flaps (paint, accent, paint) whose heights curve
  along the span, thin carbon endplates with a painted top edge; the rear wing a main plane and
  flap with a slight spoon, the DRS pod, thinner endplates; the beam wing lofted too. New: the
  diffuser (a carbon ramp behind the rear axle with strakes), the floor's upturned edges, a brake
  duct inside each wheel. The tyres are lofted with rounded shoulders (`tyreMesh`), the yellow
  band on the sidewall, a metal rim lip, the cover and the nut.
- **Damage** (Grand Prix only, as on the web): `CarDamageLook` is the web's `damageLook` (same
  numbers): the front wing in two halves hinged at the nose droops up to ~9° on the side hit, from
  80% that half breaks off (both at 100%); the rear wing tilts up to ~29° about its axle, then
  breaks off; tyres flatten from 50% (up to 35% lower, squashed onto the road). `CarPose` carries
  the state's `damage` and `velocity`; a car redraws only when its damage changes.
- **Debris** (`Render/Debris.swift`, cosmetic, the simulation never sees it): a part that breaks off
  is copied where it is and thrown with ~80% of the car's speed, kicked up 3–5.5 m/s and out to its
  side, tumbling; every hit that adds ≥3% to a wing throws 3–12 carbon and paint shards. They fall
  (9.81 m/s²), bounce (30% back), slide to a stop, stay 7 s and fade out in 1.5 s; at most 90 at
  once. A car first seen already damaged throws nothing.
- Dev: `ALTF1_DAMAGE=<fw>,<rw>,<fl>,<fr>,<rl>,<rr>` draws every car with that damage from frame
  `ALTF1_DAMAGE_AT` (later: the parts break off). Measured on Monza (snapshots, Test): ~114–120 fps
  with the pieces flying.

## Bots and the Grand Prix (X.4)

The bots (`AI/`) and the race rules (`Race/`) are ports like the model, checked against the
TypeScript (`bot-plans.json`, `bot-driving.json`: state and memory in, input and memory out, one
tick at a time, `bot-laps.json`: the qualifying laps within 2 ticks), and `GrandPrixTests` runs a
whole GP headless (ten bots, one lap of Monza, the classification, the points).

- Test has its bot again (B: Off → Easy → Medium → Hard), blue; the player red.
- The Grand Prix (`GrandPrixSession`): Singleplayer → Grand Prix → team → circuit → (São Paulo)
  weather. The team's colours (`Livery.team`), the bots' qualifying measured for their class while
  the race loads, qualifying alone (Q1–Q3; Enter ends it), the grid (1/2/3: 4, 8, 12 laps; 4 then
  ←/→ any 1–24, Enter), the F1 lights, the race with damage and mandatory stops through the drawn
  pit lane (boxes framed, the own one yellow), the results (podium, classification with team and
  points, constructors; Enter steps through, Race again, Esc). Off the track (qualifying or the race
  done) the camera follows the best placed car still on it.
- HUD in the GP: the panel (order, gaps, PIT/BOX, stops x/y, the purple stopwatch), the sector
  table (purple = the session's best of every driver), the lap as `Q2` or `P3/10 · LAP 2/8`, the
  start lights, the damage panel.
- Not yet: the cars' name tags and the flag marshal at the line (the web has both).

## Scenery (X.5)

One circuit at a time (rule 5); a circuit without its `CircuitLook` keeps the plain look (grass,
guard rails). The OSM scenery exported with the circuit (`CircuitScenery`: stands, pit lane and
buildings, woods, and on a town circuit every building, the sea, piers, pools, palms, the tunnel)
is placed by `AltF1Core/Scenery/` (`Placement`: the web's `placement.ts`; `Ground`: the real land of
an elevated circuit, settled under the road; `Triangulate`: ear clipping fast enough for Marina
Bay's 3 000-point water), built off the main thread by `SceneryGeometry` into one mesh per
material (`SceneryParts`), drawn with `SceneryMaterials` (PBR facades, glass, Belle Époque stone,
paving, water that mirrors the sky, planks, concrete, crowds) and `CircuitSceneryBuilder`. Walls take
their style by place (concrete by stands and pits, tyres by gravel, the circuit's barrier).

- **Monaco** (X.5a): the town on the real land (EU-DEM), 301 buildings at their real heights in
  Riviera pastels, glass towers, the Casino (copper mansard, towers) and the Sainte-Dévote chapel,
  Port Hercule with its piers and yachts moored stern-to, the Rainier III pool, palms, umbrella
  pines, the tunnel under the Fairmont (portals, lights), green slopes above the town and the
  hills behind it, guard rails with catch fences, paved run-off.
- **Singapore** (X.5b), a night race: a navy sky with stars and the city's glow, the floodlights'
  cool white from overhead, poles every 30 m behind the walls with glowing lamps, 1 190 buildings lit
  window by window (8 × 8 windows per texture repeat, seeded), the Singapore Flyer (lit rim, 28
  capsules), Marina Bay Sands with its SkyPark, the Esplanade's durians, the bay, palms and the
  park, darker grass, catch fences.

## Online (X.7)

The app is one more client of the web's server (`npm run server`, protocol v18): Mac and browser
players share the same rooms, nothing changes on the server. Sub-steps: **X.7a** protocol and
socket, X.7b the lobby menus (nickname → mode → circuit), X.7c online Free practice (prediction)
and X.7d the online Grand Prix done, awaiting the owner's test with friends. How friends set it up
(the project sent as it is, the host runs the server): [README](../README.md#play-together-on-your-macs).

- **Protocol** (`AltF1Core/Net/Protocol.swift`): the web's `net/protocol.ts` message for message,
  the same JSON keys; `ClientMessage.encoded()` writes what `encodeMessage` writes,
  `ServerMessage.parse` reads what the server sends (trusted, like `parseServerMessage`: unknown
  types give nil). `GameState`, `GrandPrixInfo` and the race types decode from the server's JSON
  as they are (absent and null alike). Nicknames (`Nickname`) follow `net/nickname.ts`: NFC,
  trimmed, 3–16 code points, letters of any language, digits, space, `_`, `-`; `key` for
  uniqueness.
- **Address** (`ServerAddress.url`): empty → `ws://localhost:8787/ws`; a host (LAN, Tailscale IP
  or name) → its port 8787; `host:port` → that port; the page's URL friends open in the browser
  (`http://100.64.0.7:5173`) → `ws://…:5173/ws` through Vite's proxy; `https` → `wss`.
- **Socket** (`AltF1/Net/NetClient.swift`, the web's `NetClient`): `URLSessionWebSocketTask`; open
  once the server answers a WebSocket ping (5 s timeout: `timeout`, else `unreachable`); messages
  arrive on the main actor in order; `onClose` when an open connection ends, not on `close()`.
  The app's sandbox already allows outgoing connections. **App Transport Security** refuses plain
  `ws://` to anything but localhost (`NSURLErrorDomain -1022`, found on the owner's first game over
  Tailscale): `AltF1Info.plist` sets `NSAllowsArbitraryLoads` (the server is plain ws:// on a
  private address). A failed connection shows the system's reason after "Server unreachable", and
  Debug builds log it (`NSLog`).
- **Parity**: `protocol.json` (from `fixtures.ts`) holds every client and server message as the web
  encodes them (a real Monza snapshot, both Grand Prix shapes) and ten nicknames with
  `checkNickname`/`nicknameKey`'s answers; `ProtocolTests` compares (snapshot and GP exactly).
- **Lobby** (X.7b; the web's `multiplayer/start.ts`, `lobbyText.ts`, `recordsText.ts`): Start →
  Multiplayer → **nickname screen**: the server's address (empty = this Mac; else the host's LAN or
  Tailscale IP, or the page's URL) and the nickname with the web's live hint; both remembered
  (`altf1.online.address`, `altf1.online.nickname` in the user defaults); Enter joins
  ("Connecting…", then the server's reason in red: taken, full, out of date, unreachable). Then
  **mode** (players online per mode) → **circuit** (the circuits this app knows; "GP in progress"
  disabled; in the Grand Prix the selected circuit's TOP 10 above the rows, the own one in red) →
  `enter` → the **online race** (X.7c–d, below). Esc from the
  mode or circuit menu or the room leaves the server, back to the start; a refused room (its GP just
  started) or a lost connection shows its card, Enter/Esc back to the start. `Lobby` turns the
  socket's messages into what the menus show (`@Observable`); a rejected join is shown under the
  field, never as a disconnection.
- **Prediction** (X.7c, `AltF1Core/Net/Predictor.swift`): the web's `Predictor` line for line
  (the same constants: 4 m snap, 0.35 s for the others, fade rates 10 and 6 /s, 120 inputs kept),
  its rules rebuilt with `onlineSimContext` (damage by phase, boxes, weather; tyres with the pit
  lane); `PredictorTests`: replays to the server's own state, the start hold, glide and snap.
- **The race** (`Modes/OnlineSession.swift`, `Modes/OnlineScreen.swift`; the web's `OnlineGame`
  and `OnlineGrandPrix`): every tick the numbered input goes out and moves the prediction, a ping
  once a second (F3 shows the round trip); the room's players give the liveries (the web's
  `PLAYER_LIVERIES`, `Livery.online`) and the minimap's names. `RaceSession.playerId` (the
  server's id online, "player" on this Mac) tells the renderer, the HUD and the minimap which car
  is the player's. `Lobby` keeps the room's `grandPrix` (it comes before the race is on screen) and
  hands the race's messages on (`onRaceMessage`).
- **Grand Prix online** (X.7d): `GrandPrixText.online` (the web's `grandPrixContent`: the
  manager's keys only for the manager, whom the others wait for, the rain's 1/2 in the lobby, a GP
  that started without the player); start lights from the hold; damage, tyres (1/2 in the box) and
  speed panels; off the track the camera follows the best placed car on it; the race's
  classification when it ends (`OnlineResultsView`: Enter plays again, Esc leaves). The own pit
  box's yellow follows the server's boxes live (`RaceRenderer.markOwnBox`, the web's
  `markPitBox`): the server gives a box when the player joins and may move it (play again, a
  driver leaving); painted once with the track it showed a stale box where the car never stopped
  (fixed 2026-11-24).
- **Dev**: `ALTF1_ONLINE=<address>,<nickname>,<practice|grandPrix>,<circuit name>` joins and
  enters at launch (with `ALTF1_AUTOPILOT`, `ALTF1_SNAPSHOTS`).
- **Measured** (2026-11-23, a local server and a second player from a script): practice on Monza
  with both cars; a Grand Prix on Monza and Monaco with the other player as manager: lobby
  (waiting for the manager), qualifying, grid, the start lights, the race (stops required), the
  classification with Play again.
- **Measured** (2026-11-23, local server): connect, `hello` → `welcome` (14 rooms, 7 record
  boards), `ping` → `pong`, `enter` Monza's Grand Prix → `entered`, `grandPrix` (lobby, the probe
  as manager), snapshots at 30 Hz with the inputs acknowledged (tick 60: seq 56); an address with
  no server → `unreachable`.

## The model (X.1)

A 1:1 port of `01_packages/shared/src/{math,config,types,track,sim}`: the same functions under the
same names (`stepGame`, `buildTrack`, `locateOnTrack`, `resolveCollisions`, `placePuddles`…), the
same order of operations. Values are `struct`s (`Codable`, `Sendable`), so `GameState` copies like
the web's spread objects and encodes to the same JSON keys (the protocol, X.7). The web's
`CAR_PARAMS`-style constants are `CarParams.baseline` etc.; `Record<Surface, …>` is `SurfaceTable`.

JavaScript semantics kept on purpose (`Math/Angle.swift`): `jsSign` (sign of 0 is 0), `jsRound`
(halves round up), `jsMod` (`%` on numbers), `jsHypot` (**V8's `Math.hypot`**: normalised by the
larger value and Kahan-summed, not libm's `hypot`); ids sorted by UTF-16 code units; the impacts in
first-met order; the puddles' PRNG on wrapping 32-bit integers (`Math.imul`).

## Parity

`01_tools/swiftExport/fixtures.ts` records what the TypeScript computes and the Swift tests compare:

- `tracks.json`: every circuit compiled with its pit lane (every 7th sample and wall, every kerb
  and run-off) and 200 points round it (`locateOnTrack`, `surfaceAt`);
- `puddles.json`: Monza's and São Paulo's puddles;
- `protocol.json`: the multiplayer messages and nicknames ([Online](#online-x7));
- `sim-<scenario>.json`: ticks of three races (state, inputs, next state; one in 30 plus every lap,
  sector, stop, reset and contact tick): **Monza** (4 cars in traffic, contacts, a reset, a full
  lap), **Monaco** (heights, damage, pit lane, limiter, a stop and its repair), **São Paulo**
  (rain and puddles, damage, pit lane). Swift steps each recorded state once and compares: one tick
  at a time, so differences never pile up.

Results (2026-11-22): the compiled tracks, their locations and surfaces and the puddles are
**bit-identical**; a tick of `stepGame` differs at most by 1 ulp (≈1e-16, from `sin`/`cos`/`exp`:
Darwin's libm vs V8's fdlibm). The tests allow 1e-9 relative. Re-run the tool when the web's model
changes; the tests then show what the Swift port must follow.

## Circuit data

`01_tools/swiftExport/export.ts` imports `GP_TRACKS`, the generated scenery (`<id>Layout.ts`) and
land (`<id>Land.ts`) and writes per circuit `Circuits/<id>.json`:

- `id`, `definition` (the `TrackDefinition` as is: `points` of `{ at: { x, z }, width, height? }`,
  `pitLane`, `sectorPoints`, …),
- `scenery`: the layout's exports without their prefix, in camelCase (`MONACO_PIT_BUILDINGS` →
  `pitBuildings`); `TrackSpot`s stay `[t, offset]` pairs, `Vec2`s `{ x, z }`,
- `land`: the height grid of an elevated circuit (`minX`, `minZ`, `cell`, `cols`, `rows`,
  `heights`), else `null`;

and `Circuits/index.json`: `{ id, name, elevated, length }` in `GP_TRACKS` order (length: the
compiled lap's, m, for the menus). Re-run it whenever a
circuit or its scenery is regenerated. It only reads the web code and needs no `npm install`.

## Conventions

- Same units and axes as the web (metres, seconds, radians; ground `{ x, z }`, heading 0 = −Z):
  RealityKit is Y-up like Three.js, so no conversion layer.
- Keys are physical (`NSEvent.keyCode`), as the web's `KeyboardEvent.code`.
- Swift 6 with main-actor default isolation in the app; `AltF1Core` stays free of actors (plain
  value types, `Sendable`).
- Deployment target macOS 26 (Liquid Glass).
